4 Steps to Transformation

Select your source material and consider what might work well as an interactive element before uploading it.

  • Identify the specific text, transcript, or worksheet section.
  • Decide which parts of the document might be useful as interactive components.
  • Upload the file into your chosen AI workspace.

Be Aware: Before uploading or adapting existing materials, check that you have permission to use them in this way. Copyright or licence terms may limit copying, adaptation, or redistribution.

Avoid uploading confidential or personally identifiable information, and review the terms of the AI tool you are using.

Review the resulting lesson carefully before using or sharing it. Check the content, answers, examples, images, and activities for accuracy, appropriateness, accessibility, and unintended bias.

Write a prompt suggesting how the application might function and who it is for.

  • Suggest the activity type (e.g., matching, clickable quiz).
  • Define the target learner demographic.
  • Ask the AI to include specific interactions or feedback styles.

Test the generated draft yourself and issue follow-up prompts to adjust features or fix issues.

  • Click through the app to verify if the logic and buttons work as intended.
  • Check if distractor options are plausible but clearly wrong.
  • You can ask the AI to iterate (e.g., "Try adding a score counter").

Export the final code into a usable format and distribute it, provided you have permission to do so.

  • Copy the generated HTML code if it meets your needs.
  • Host the single file using simple platforms or embed it into your LMS.
  • Share the final URL with your learners.

Keep the First Prompt Simple

You do not need to describe everything at once. Start with a basic conversion, then test the result and ask the AI to make adjustments.

"Try adding a scoring system." "Change the multiple-choice activity to drag and drop." "Make the instructions simpler." "Include immediate feedback after answers." "Can you adapt the text for B1 learners?" "Make the text larger on mobile screens."

Ethical & Copyright Considerations

Practical safeguards to consider before uploading source materials or distributing an interactive lesson.

Ownership & Rights

Permission & Licensing

Using AI does not confer copyright clearance. Check that you hold the rights to the original material or that its license terms (e.g., Creative Commons, Open Educational Resources) permit digital adaptation and redistribution.

Data Protection

Student Privacy & PII

Never upload documents containing personally identifiable information (PII), student names, grades, or proprietary institutional records. Review the privacy policies and training opt-outs of your chosen AI tool.

Human Review

Accuracy & Validation

AI-generated answer keys, feedback explanations, and distractor options may contain plausible-sounding errors. Always test the generated activity and verify all factual claims before learners see them.

Fairness

Bias & Accessibility

Examine examples and dialogues for cultural assumptions or unexamined stereotypes. Confirm that color combinations maintain readable contrast and that interactive elements remain usable with assistive technologies.

Core Principle: AI is a drafting assistant, not an educational authority. The educator retains responsibility for the pedagogical validity, licensing rights, and ethical appropriateness of all shared materials.
