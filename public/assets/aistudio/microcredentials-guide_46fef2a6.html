Microcredentials: Small Steps in Professional Learning

You do not always need a long qualification to develop a new area of expertise. Focused courses and microcredentials can help you build specific skills and show how your practice is developing.

What is a microcredential? A microcredential is a focused recognition of learning in a specific skill or competency. It usually involves clear learning outcomes and some form of assessment, so it shows more than simply attending a course.

What makes it useful?

Focused learning

Develop one area at a time, such as teaching pronunciation, designing online lessons, or using assessment effectively.

Evidence of learning

Show what you have learned through a quiz, task, project, reflection, or practical demonstration.

Build over time

Collect relevant learning experiences to shape a professional profile that reflects your interests and goals.

One important distinction: Not every short online course or completion certificate is a microcredential. Some providers offer professional development courses, while others have a dedicated microcredential programme. Check how each provider describes and assesses its offer.

These examples offer different ways to continue learning. FREE and PAID labels indicate whether access to the listed programmes requires payment. Course formats and certificate requirements can change.

Online Professional English Network (OPEN) MOOCs

FREE

Before you enrol, check four things

1 Who issues it?

Check the institution or organisation behind the programme. Look for clear information about its expertise and reputation.

2 What will you learn?

Look for specific outcomes. “Improve teaching” is broad; “design an online speaking task” is clearer.

3 How is learning assessed?

Find out whether you complete a quiz, submit an assignment, build a resource, or demonstrate a skill.

4 Where will it take you?

Check the workload, certificate details, recognition, and whether the learning is relevant to your next step.

Turn learning into professional growth

Identify a gap

For example, you want to make your online lessons more interactive.

Choose one focus

Find a course or microcredential that addresses that specific need.

Apply the learning

Try a new technique with your learners and collect evidence of what happens.

Reflect and share

Keep the certificate, a sample resource, and a short reflection in your portfolio.

Choose learning with a purpose.

A credential is most valuable when it connects to a real teaching need. Start with a skill you want to develop, look for credible learning, and make time to apply it in your own context.
