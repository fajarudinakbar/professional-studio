Microcredentials: Small Steps in Professional Learning

You do not always need a long qualification to develop a new area of expertise. Focused courses and microcredentials can help you build specific skills and show how your practice is developing.

What is a microcredential?

A microcredential is a focused recognition of learning in a specific skill or competency. It usually involves clear learning outcomes and some form of assessment, so it shows more than simply attending a course.

1

What Makes Microcredentials Useful?

Focused Learning

Develop one area at a time, such as teaching pronunciation, designing online lessons, or using assessment effectively.

Evidence of Learning

Show what you have learned through a quiz, task, project, reflection, or practical demonstration.

Build Over Time

Collect relevant learning experiences to shape a professional profile that reflects your interests and goals.

One important distinction: Not every short online course or completion certificate is a microcredential. Some providers offer professional development courses, while others have a dedicated microcredential programme. Check how each provider describes and assesses its offer.

Before You Enrol: Check Four Things

Interactive evaluation guide for teachers

1

Who issues it?

Check the institution or organisation behind the programme. Look for clear information about its expertise, accreditation, and reputation in ELT.

2

What will you learn?

Look for specific learning outcomes. “Improve teaching” is too broad; “design an interactive online speaking task” is clear and actionable.

3

How is learning assessed?

Find out whether you complete a quiz, submit an assignment, build a practical resource, or demonstrate a classroom skill.

4

Where will it take you?

Check the workload, certificate details, career recognition, and whether the learning directly connects to your next professional step.

4

CORE TAKEAWAY

Choose Learning with a Purpose
